# Public discovery UX/QA — 8 October 2026

This pass makes the public website easier to understand and explore. It is a frontend/copy improvement, not a fresh certification of all application workflows or an engagement/conversion study. Public documentation contains no account credentials, private identifiers or private evidence references.

## What changed

- Homepage: one primary expert-browse action, a short guide anchor, clearer introductory copy and a distinction between free client membership and session prices.
- Six compact goal/topic links reuse the existing category routes. A three-step guide describes profile discovery, slot choice and a request awaiting confirmation, not an instantly confirmed meeting.
- Native expandable answers explain browsing without an account, login before requesting a meeting, session conditions and invitation-only expert onboarding. Expert interest leads to contacts.
- Expert discovery excludes profiles without a selected topic-keyword match, retains useful short signals such as AI/CV and avoids generic skills tokens matching unrelated topics. Paid-tier priority stays within the matching filters and is explained.
- The no-op leading-profile toggle is removed. The rating filter explicitly means reviewed profiles rated 4.5+, exposes its pressed state and does not confuse a paid visibility badge with reviews. Matching labels are qualitative, not unsupported percentage claims.
- Search/city fields remain drafts until form submission, so typing alone does not trigger catalogue requests. Trimmed submitted filters live in the URL; result counts and empty-state recovery make the current search visible.

The existing brand, animation, live API-backed catalogue and labelled demonstration record are preserved. No new dependency, media asset, backend route, AWS service or recurring job was added. No email, login, account, booking, membership or payment state was changed for this pass.
